# Brand

## Name and voice

**Earshot** — the distance within which something can be heard.

Tagline: **Place a sound. Hear it there.**

Earshot should feel like a well-made instrument: calm, precise and quiet about itself. Write plainly. Say what a control does, not how clever it is. Prefer "Drop a sound anywhere around the listener" to "Unleash immersive 3D audio".

## Logo

![Earshot logo](../assets/logo.svg)

The mark is a radar seen from above: a solid dot for the listener, one near ring, and an outer ring broken by a single sound at the upper right. The gap in the outer ring makes the sound read as an event on the ring rather than decoration, and gives the mark a direction.

| File                        | Use                                                                                         |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `assets/logo.svg`           | Full-colour mark on its dark rounded tile. The default.                                     |
| `assets/logo-mono.svg`      | One colour, taking `currentColor`. For print, light backgrounds, watermarks.                |
| `assets/favicon.svg`        | Heavier strokes so the rings survive at 16 px. Also `favicon-32.png` and `favicon-180.png`. |
| `assets/hero.svg`           | README banner.                                                                              |
| `assets/social-preview.png` | 1280 × 640 image for GitHub's social preview.                                               |

All of them are generated by `npm run brand` from one description of the mark in `scripts/generate-brand-assets.ts`. Change the mark there, not in the output files.

Rules of thumb:

- Keep clear space around the mark of at least the radius of the inner ring.
- Do not rotate it. The sound sits at the upper right.
- In the mono version the sound is the same colour as the rings; do not recolour parts of it.
- The wordmark is "Earshot" in the system UI font at weight 650 with slightly tight tracking, set to the right of the mark.

## Colour

One accent, used sparingly, on restrained greys.

### Signal Amber

The accent is a warm amber. It marks the thing you are working with (the selected source, its spoke, its readout) and the one primary action on screen (Export). It was chosen over the expected radar green for three reasons: green-on-black is the cliché for anything shaped like a radar; amber is the colour of indicator lamps on audio equipment, which is the family Earshot belongs to; and it stays legible for people with red–green colour blindness against both the grey dots and the red used for destructive actions.

If everything is amber, nothing is. Unselected sources are neutral grey.

## Type

- **Interface:** the system UI font (`system-ui`). No web fonts are loaded.
- **Numbers:** the system monospace font with tabular figures, for times, angles, distances and levels, so values do not jitter as they change.
- **Labels on the instrument:** small capitals with wide tracking (FRONT, BEHIND, section headings).

Base size is 13 px. Weight carries hierarchy; size changes are small.

## Shape and motion

- Radii are small: 6 px for controls, 8 px for panels.
- Hairlines are 1 px. Depth comes from a single soft shadow on floating panels only.
- Motion is short (120–200 ms) and functional. The one ambient animation is the pulse on a dot while it is sounding. Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.
